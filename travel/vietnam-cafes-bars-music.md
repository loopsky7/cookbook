## Hanoi

*Hà Nội · the capital, in the north*

Hanoi rewards slow days and late nights, most of it within a walk of Hoàn Kiếm Lake. Start with egg coffee up a dim staircase, drink bia hơi on plastic stools at dusk, then choose between cocktails at two of Asia's 50 Best Bars and saxophone at the long-running Binh Minh Jazz Club. It is also the best city in Vietnam to hear northern traditional music, from ca trù to the water-puppet orchestra.

### Hanoi: Cafés (Cà phê)

Hanoi takes its coffee strong, sweet and slow: dripped through a phin onto condensed milk, drunk on low plastic stools facing the street, or topped with the whipped egg foam invented here in 1946, when milk was scarce. Beyond the family institutions serving egg, yogurt and coconut coffee, a young wave of roasters is showing what Vietnamese beans can do, from Fine Robusta in ceramic phins to flights of Sơn La and Đà Lạt arabica. The city also hides its best seats up dim staircases behind silk shops, in 1930s French villas and Soviet-era housing blocks, and on rooftops and balconies over Hoàn Kiếm Lake and Long Biên Bridge.

#### Egg coffee & classics

- **★ Café Giảng** · Old Quarter (Hoàn Kiếm) · $ · *checked 2026*
  - This is where egg coffee was born. In 1946, with milk scarce, Metropole Hotel bartender Nguyễn Văn Giảng whipped egg yolk with sugar and condensed milk over strong coffee, and his family still serves it down this Old Quarter alley. The custard-thick foam of the original is the yardstick for every copy in town.
  - Tip: Order the hot egg coffee (cà phê trứng nóng), which arrives standing in a bowl of hot water, or the egg cocoa if you like it sweeter. Go between 9:30 and 11:00 on a weekday to dodge the queues. The alley original has far more atmosphere than the Tràng Tiền and Lotte Mall branches.
  - Hours: 7:00–22:00 daily (Nguyễn Hữu Huân, per official site)
  - Address: 39 Nguyễn Hữu Huân (down a narrow alley); branches at 57 Tràng Tiền and Lotte Mall West Lake, 3rd floor
  - Price: Egg coffee about 35–60k VND (2025–26 sources vary; a Mar 2026 guide quotes ~35k hot, ~40k iced)
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cafe%20Giang%2C%2039%20Nguy%E1%BB%85n%20H%E1%BB%AFu%20Hu%C3%A2n%2C%20Ho%C3%A0n%20Ki%E1%BA%BFm%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://cafegiang.vn)
- **★ Café Đinh** · Hoàn Kiếm Lake, east shore (Hoàn Kiếm) · $ · *checked 2026*
  - Mrs Bích, daughter of egg-coffee inventor Nguyễn Văn Giảng, opened this upstairs café in 1987, and it still serves the family's 1946 recipe; plenty of fans rate its lighter foam above Giảng's. A dim staircase leads to a worn, photo-hung room whose tiny balcony looks straight out over Hoàn Kiếm Lake.
  - Tip: Find the narrow passage between the shopfronts at No. 13 and climb to the 2nd floor. The balcony holds only a handful of people, so come before 9am or in the late afternoon. If you've already had egg coffee at Giảng, order egg cocoa (cacao trứng) here.
  - Hours: About 7:00–22:00 daily (2025–26 listings)
  - Address: 2nd floor, 13 Đinh Tiên Hoàng
  - Price: Egg coffee about 30–55k VND (2025–26 sources vary)
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cafe%20Dinh%2C%2013%20%C4%90inh%20Ti%C3%AAn%20Ho%C3%A0ng%2C%20Ho%C3%A0n%20Ki%E1%BA%BFm%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://www.asiatouradvisor.com/get-inspired/vietnam/hanoi-egg-coffee-on-cnn-the-secret-of-a-70-year-old-coffee/)
- **Café Duy Trí** · Yên Phụ, between Trúc Bạch and West Lake · $ · *likely open*
  - One family has run this café since 1936 in a narrow three-storey house with ceilings so low you have to stoop, and it roasts its own beans by the door. It's Hanoi's reference for yogurt coffee (sữa chua cà phê): house-made yogurt is semi-frozen and phin coffee is dripped over it, so it's closer to a coffee slushie than a drink.
  - Tip: Order sữa chua cà phê, since the semi-frozen yogurt is the whole point, and climb to the top floor. Bring cash. Search the café's name on Google Maps rather than trusting listicle addresses, as some 2026 lists wrongly put it on Từ Hoa.
  - Hours: About 6:00–22:00 (Google-sourced listing)
  - Address: 43A Yên Phụ
  - Price: Yogurt coffee about 30k VND (recent guide); 25–40k is typical at traditional cafés
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cafe%20Duy%20Tri%2C%2043A%20Y%C3%AAn%20Ph%E1%BB%A5%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://saigoneer.com/hanoi-street-food-restaurants/21341-h%E1%BA%BBm-gems-duy-tri-one-of-hanoi%E2%80%99s-original-cafes)
- **Café Lâm** · Old Quarter (Hoàn Kiếm) · $ · *checked 2025*
  - Since 1952 this has been where Hanoi's painters, poets and musicians drink. Founder Nguyễn Văn Lâm gave struggling artists such as Bùi Xuân Phái and Văn Cao coffee on credit. Work by Phái, Nguyễn Sáng and Dương Bích Liên still hangs above a dark-roast phin that has barely changed in decades.
  - Tip: Order a cà phê đen or nâu (black or milk phin) and take a street-side stool to watch Nguyễn Hữu Huân go by; Giảng is a few doors along at No. 39. There's no air-con, and evenings bring mosquitoes.
  - Hours: 7:00–22:00 (listings)
  - Address: 60 Nguyễn Hữu Huân
  - Price: 15–50k VND (Foody listing)
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cafe%20Lam%2C%2060%20Nguy%E1%BB%85n%20H%E1%BB%AFu%20Hu%C3%A2n%2C%20Ho%C3%A0n%20Ki%E1%BA%BFm%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://www.tatlerasia.com/dining/drinks/4-hanoi-legendary-coffee-shops-for-politicians-vn)
- **Cộng Cà Phê (Trích Sài, West Lake)** (Cộng Cà Phê) · West Lake (Tây Hồ) · $$ · *checked 2026*
  - Cộng turned cốt dừa cà phê, strong coffee blended with icy coconut-milk slush, into a national staple, served amid army-green, subsidy-era (bao cấp) décor. This roomy, light-filled branch on the West Lake shore road faces the water and makes a dependable sunset stop.
  - Tip: Order cốt dừa cà phê (add 'ít ngọt' for less sweet) and arrive for the golden half-hour before sunset. In the centre, the 27 Nhà Thờ branch by the Cathedral pours the same drink. Older guides still send you to the 2007 original at 152D Triệu Việt Vương or to 35A Nguyễn Hữu Huân, but neither is on Cộng's 2026 store list. West Lake road-widening (2026–30) may bring roadworks on Trích Sài.
  - Hours: 7:15–23:00 daily (Cộng store list)
  - Address: 75 Trích Sài (central branches: 27 Nhà Thờ, 116 Cầu Gỗ, 5 Trần Phú)
  - Price: Coconut coffee about 55–70k VND (2026 guides)
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=C%E1%BB%99ng%20C%C3%A0%20Ph%C3%AA%2C%2075%20Tr%C3%ADch%20S%C3%A0i%2C%20T%C3%A2y%20H%E1%BB%93%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://congcaphe.com/stores)

#### Specialty roasters

- **★ RAAW Coffee (Hàng Buồm)** · Old Quarter (Hoàn Kiếm) · $$ · *checked 2026*
  - Founded in 2019 by Q Arabica Grader Nguyễn Phương Linh, RAAW roasts only Vietnamese coffee bought directly from smallholders: Sơn La arabica, Đà Lạt Honey Typica, Đắk Lắk fine robusta, even Quảng Trị liberica. Its two airy floors on Hàng Buồm are the best single stop in Hanoi for tasting what Vietnam's own specialty beans can do.
  - Tip: Order the Phượt Set ('Vietnam Road Trip'), four Vietnamese coffees side by side, and ask to try the liberica (cà phê mít), Vietnam's rare third species. Because it's open until 11pm, it's the specialty bar that still works after dinner. Its 2-hour English brewing class (phin, V60, AeroPress, syphon; morning slots, max 10 people) is booked through Airbnb Experiences.
  - Hours: 8:00–23:00 daily (official site)
  - Address: 15 Hàng Buồm (roastery café: B3.7 Hoàng Đạo Thúy, Thanh Xuân)
  - Price: Beans 108–216k VND a bag; brewing class about US$35 per person (Airbnb listing)
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=RAAW%20Coffee%2C%2015%20H%C3%A0ng%20Bu%E1%BB%93m%2C%20Ho%C3%A0n%20Ki%E1%BA%BFm%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://raawcoffee.com/hanoi-hang-buom)
- **★ refined. specialty coffee** · St Joseph's Cathedral (Hoàn Kiếm) · $$ · *checked 2026*
  - Vũ Đình Tú opened refined. in 2021 to defend Vietnamese robusta. It brews specialty-grade Fine Robusta through its own ceramic phin filters, made in the Bát Tràng pottery village. The all-black slow bar tucked into a menswear store by the Cathedral is where Hanoi's coffee nerds find out how good a phin can be.
  - Tip: Start with a black Fine Robusta through the ceramic phin, then have an arabica pour-over to compare. Some listings give the address as 5 Nhà Thờ, so search 'refined. specialty coffee' on Google Maps. refined.coffee is not the café's website.
  - Address: 17 Nhà Thờ, Hàng Trống (slow bar inside the No Concept store); original Phin bar near Văn Miếu
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=refined.%20specialty%20coffee%2C%20Nh%C3%A0%20Th%E1%BB%9D%2C%20Ho%C3%A0n%20Ki%E1%BA%BFm%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://sprudge.com/in-hanoi-roaster-retailer-cafes-offer-a-glimpse-into-vietnams-changing-coffee-culture-468966.html)
- **Donkey Coffee Roastery** · Red River bank, Chương Dương (Hoàn Kiếm) · $$ · *checked 2026*
  - In a quiet alley on the Red River bank, with a shady garden and DIY industrial-vintage décor, a former graphic designer roasts a wide range of Vietnamese and imported specialty lots, including a natural-processed Gesha from neighbouring Laos. Its regular public cuppings are the easiest way to taste alongside Hanoi's coffee crowd.
  - Tip: Check the café's social pages for the next public cupping. Ask for whatever natural or Gesha lot is on the pour-over menu; in summer, the orange cold brew is the crowd favourite. Search by name on Maps, because the ward now shows as Hồng Hà.
  - Address: 37 ngõ 139 Chương Dương Độ (Hồng Hà ward)
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Donkey%20Coffee%20Roastery%2C%2037%20ng%C3%B5%20139%20Ch%C6%B0%C6%A1ng%20D%C6%B0%C6%A1ng%20%C4%90%E1%BB%99%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://sprudge.com/in-hanoi-roaster-retailer-cafes-offer-a-glimpse-into-vietnams-changing-coffee-culture-468966.html)
- **VULAB Specialty Coffee** · French Quarter edge, off Trần Hưng Đạo (Hoàn Kiếm) · $$ · *checked 2026*
  - Pharmacist-turned-roaster Lê Quang Vũ, a Q Grader with SCA sensory certification, opened this lab-like white-and-grey bar with a small courtyard in 2023. Beans run from Vietnamese highland lots to Panama, Costa Rica and Ethiopia, tiered from 'common' to 'legendary', and are brewed on syphon, phin, AeroPress or moka pot.
  - Tip: Choose a higher-tier lot on syphon and take it out to the courtyard. ACID8 (34 Xóm Hà Hồi), a red-walled in-house roaster known for its whipped, foam-capped iced Americano, is a few doors down the same lane, so it's an easy two-stop crawl.
  - Address: 30B Xóm Hà Hồi, Trần Hưng Đạo
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=VULAB%20Specialty%20Coffee%2C%2030B%20X%C3%B3m%20H%C3%A0%20H%E1%BB%93i%2C%20Ho%C3%A0n%20Ki%E1%BA%BFm%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://www.thewaytocoffee.com/hanoi/)
- **30S Coffea Roastery** · Kim Liên collective housing (Kim Liên ward, formerly Đống Đa) · $$ · *checked 2026*
  - With Cư Xá closed since August 2026, this is the best way to drink coffee inside a real Soviet-era tập thể. The roastery sits in one of Hanoi's oldest collective-housing estates, and its brew counter wraps around the roaster so you drink while watching the roasting. The line-up includes a Fine Robusta ranked Top 1 at the Vietnam Amazing Cup 2025.
  - Tip: Ask for the award-winning Fine Robusta as a pour-over (Sprudge notes wine, tropical fruit, nut and brown sugar), then wander the estate's stairwells and courtyards. The founder, who has more than ten years in the trade, also runs barista training; message ahead to ask about English.
  - Address: 108B6 Kim Liên collective housing (tập thể Kim Liên), ngõ 43 Phạm Ngọc Thạch
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=30S%20Coffea%20Roastery%2C%20ng%C3%B5%2043%20Ph%E1%BA%A1m%20Ng%E1%BB%8Dc%20Th%E1%BA%A1ch%2C%20Kim%20Li%C3%AAn%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://sprudge.com/in-hanoi-roaster-retailer-cafes-offer-a-glimpse-into-vietnams-changing-coffee-culture-468966.html)
- **Kafeville** · Trúc Bạch Lake (Ba Đình) · $$ · *likely open*
  - Opened in March 2016, Kafeville was Hanoi's first café built around in-house-roasted pour-overs. It is still a calm, coffee-first room where the loudest sounds are the grinder and the scale.
  - Tip: Order a V60 of a light-roast single origin (Ethiopia, Kenya and Costa Rica sit alongside Đà Lạt on the bean list), then walk round Trúc Bạch Lake; Duy Trí's yogurt coffee on Yên Phụ is close by. It closes at 6pm.
  - Hours: 8:00–18:00 daily (Kafeville website)
  - Address: 23 Yên Ninh, Trúc Bạch (also 101-B3 Trần Huy Liệu, Giảng Võ)
  - Price: Retail beans about 60–170k VND per 100g (Vietcetera)
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kafeville%2C%2023%20Y%C3%AAn%20Ninh%2C%20Ba%20%C4%90%C3%ACnh%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://www.kafeville.com/about)

  - You walk through a silk shop and down a lantern-lit passage into a century-old family house with a courtyard of antiques, songbirds and an ancestral altar. Narrow stairs then climb to a rooftop that looks across Hoàn Kiếm Lake. This is the classic Hanoi café behind a secret staircase.
  - Tip: Head straight for the top floor and order egg coffee or a phin. Arrive before 9am or in the hour before sunset for a terrace seat and the best light. The foam here is a little sweeter than Giảng's.

### Hanoi: Bars (Quán bar)

Hanoi drinks at two speeds. On the street, Hanoians sink same-day bia hơi at about 10,000–15,000 đồng a glass on plastic stools. Up the stairs and down hidden alleys, a cocktail scene built on Vietnamese ingredients now has two bars on Asia's 50 Best Bars 2026: Workshop14 at #34 and The Hudson Rooms at #46. Add colonial-era hotel bars with a century of stories, sunset terraces over West Lake and Hoàn Kiếm Lake, and a new wave of natural-wine bars, and every budget is covered.

#### Cocktail bars & speakeasies

- **★ Workshop14** · West Lake (Tây Hồ) · $$$ · *checked 2026*
  - Vietnam's top-ranked bar, opened in early 2025, is #34 on Asia's 50 Best Bars 2026 and won both the Best New Opening and Best Bar in Vietnam awards. In a calm, wood-clad former architect's studio by West Lake, Rich McDonough (ex-69 Colebrooke Row) uses vacuum distillation and clarification on Vietnamese produce.
  - Tip: Book ahead (+84 934 446 669 or Instagram), allow time, and ask for the veranda. Try Can't Stand the Heat, a margarita made with vacuum-redistilled Vietnamese chilli that keeps the flavour but drops the burn, or Terry's Chocolate Orange (cognac infused with Marou cacao nibs). Start with a golden-hour drink at the InterContinental's Sunset Bar, a short walk along Từ Hoa.
  - Hours: Daily, evenings (exact times not published)
  - Address: 6 Ngõ 5 Từ Hoa, Tây Hồ, Hanoi
  - Price: Average cocktail about US$12 (roughly 300k VND), per Asia's 50 Best
  - [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Workshop14%2C%206%20Ng%C3%B5%205%20T%E1%BB%AB%20Hoa%2C%20T%C3%A2y%20H%E1%BB%93%2C%20Hanoi) · [Source](https://www.the50.com/bars/best-in-asia/awards/best-new-opening.html)
- **★ The Haflington** · Old Quarter (Hoàn Kiếm) · $$$ · *checked 2026*
  - The bar that put Hanoi on the cocktail map: in 2024 it became the first Vietnamese bar in Asia's 50 Best Bars top 50 (#47). A narrow Old Quarter corridor leads into a two-floor 'natural history museum' of fossils, old books and a dinosaur skeleton hanging over the bar.
  - Tip: Book weekend evenings well ahead, because tables are very hard to get; staff meet you on Hàng Mã and walk you in. Try the Rabbit's Habit (mezcal, Green Chartreuse, carrot, cinnamon) or the Coin Flipper (rye, Fernet-Branca, chocolate bitters). The crowd is now mostly tourists. It fell to #87 in 2025 and is not on the 2026 list.
  - Hours: Daily from late afternoon or evening until about 1am (listings differ on opening: 3pm or 6pm)

### Hanoi: Music (Âm nhạc)

Hanoi is Vietnam's jazz capital and the heartland of northern folk music: within a short walk of Hoàn Kiếm Lake you can hear Quyền Văn Minh's saxophone in the country's first jazz club, young players in hidden listening rooms, and centuries-old ca trù and water-puppet orchestras. After dark, the underground has regrouped around a few clubs since Savage and Hanoi Rock City closed, while phòng trà and Trịnh Công Sơn cafés keep Vietnamese song alive. With the colonial Opera House shut for restoration, the big classical nights have moved to the new Hồ Gươm Opera.

### Hanoi: local tips

- Order like a local: milk coffee is 'nâu' (iced: 'nâu đá'), black is 'đen' or 'đen đá', and 'bạc xỉu' is the milkiest. Egg coffee is 'cà phê trứng', yogurt coffee 'sữa chua cà phê' and coconut coffee 'cốt dừa cà phê'; add 'ít ngọt' for less sweet. Hot egg coffee arrives standing in a bowl of hot water, so leave it there and eat the foam with the spoon.
- 2026 prices: at street cafés, đen đá costs 20–35k VND and nâu 25–40k. Egg coffee is about 35–60k at the heritage cafés, drinks at design and view cafés run 50–90k, and a specialty pour-over is about 70–150k. Carry cash for the old family cafés; tipping isn't expected.
- Hidden cafés sit behind shops, down corridors and up dim stairwells, so look for small signs at alley mouths. 'Tầng 2' means the 2nd floor, 'ngõ' a lane and 'tập thể' a collective housing block, and alley signs ask you to push your bike with the engine off. Since the mid-2025 ward merger, official addresses use new ward names, so search Maps by café name or by house number plus street.
- Timing: for lake, balcony and rooftop seats, go before 9am or in the hour before sunset; weekend evenings are the busiest. To skip the queue at Giảng, go between 9:30 and 11:00 on a weekday. Most specialty bars open around 8am, but Kafeville and Ta Cà Phê close at 6pm, while RAAW Hàng Buồm stays open until 11pm.
- Easy crawls: Nguyễn Hữu Huân has Giảng (No. 39) and Lâm (No. 60). Hàng Hành lane by the lake is lined with pavement cafés, including Nhân (39D), a 1946 institution. VULAB (30B) and ACID8 (34) are a few doors apart on Xóm Hà Hồi, off Trần Hưng Đạo. Around the Cathedral, refined., Loading T, Blackbird and Miên Man are all within a five-minute walk.
- Taste Vietnam side by side: try a Fine Robusta through a phin (refined., 30S Coffea) against a Sơn La or Đà Lạt arabica on V60 (RAAW, Ta Cà Phê), plus liberica (cà phê mít) at RAAW. Bags of Vietnamese specialty beans (about 60–220k VND) make light souvenirs. Skip souvenir-shop 'weasel coffee', an overpriced novelty that raises animal-welfare concerns.
- Hanoi's café scene churns, and older lists still send people to places that are gone: Cư Xá (closed 31 Aug 2026), Trill Rooftop (closed Oct 2025), Hip Hub (closed) and the Highlands in the Shark Jaw building (demolished 2025). Check recent Google Maps reviews or Facebook posts before crossing town.
- Train Street (Oct 2026): the trackside cafés are illegal but tolerated. Tour companies have been barred since March 2025, police clear the tracks periodically, and there have been fewer trains since late February 2026. If you go anyway, buy a drink, sit against the wall, never stand on the rails, and ignore 'train in 5 minutes' hustles; ask to see the timetable.
- How bia hơi works: it is a light (about 4%) unpasteurised lager brewed and delivered daily, so it is drunk the same day. The peak is the 4–7pm after-work rush, and most bia hơi places close by 10–11pm. Staff keep count and you pay when you leave, usually in cash. Toast with 'Một, hai, ba, dzô!' and order lạc rang, đậu rán or nem chua rán.
- Happy hours make the big names affordable. The Hudson Rooms: 2-for-1 5:30–7:30pm. angelina at the Metropole: free second drink 4–7pm. Polite & Co: 5–8pm. Terraço: 30% off 5–7pm. Sunset Bar: 2-for-1 'Làng' cocktails 6–8pm. Several deals are per person, not per table.
- Read the small print. Hotel prices marked '++' add about 15% (5% service plus 10% VAT on the Summit and Metropole menus), while USEbar and The Haflington include VAT and service. Expect about 200–450k VND for a serious cocktail.
- Sunset falls around 5:15–6pm from October to February and about 6:40pm in June–July. Be seated 30–45 minutes early at The Summit, Terraço or Sunset Bar, and book weekend sunset tables. Haze can mute the views, so make the most of clear days.

## Sa Pa

*Sa Pa · mountain town under Fansipan, far north*

Sa Pa trades big-city scenes for altitude and cold: dawn coffee above a sea of cloud, village cafés along the Mường Hoa valley trek, and fireside bars pouring táo mèo (wild-apple) wine when the mist comes down. Its signature sound is the Hmong khèn, played free every night by young dancers in the town centre, in hourly shows at Cát Cát and at Fansipan's Bản Mây, and at the Saturday Love Market. Between October 2026 and March 2027 the winter and snow festivals, Christmas and New Year on the church square, and Tết's village rites add more.

### Sa Pa: local tips

- Saturday is the big music night. The Love Market fills the Stone Church square from about 19:30, the free khèn dancers play at Sân Quần every night from about 20:00 to 22:00, and the weekend night market's folk stage runs Fri–Sun, liveliest 20:00–22:00. Color Bar adds live leaf and bamboo flute on Tuesdays and Fridays; other weeknights are quiet.
- Clouds and cold: cloud-sea season is roughly Sep–Nov (early October is prime), so be at a view café or on Fansipan before about 9:00. Oct–Mar is the dry season but it's cold, with Dec–Jan lows in town around 6–7°C and the Fansipan summit below 0°C in cold snaps, and many view cafés are open-air. For warm indoor seats go to Cacao, Le Gecko or Sapa Center Cafe (12 Cầu Mây), which has a log fire.
- Fansipan's cable car reopened on 28 Sep 2026 after maintenance. The last car up leaves at 14:30 Mon–Thu and Sun and 15:30 Fri–Sat, and the last car down at 16:00 or 17:00. Hours change with the weather, so check sunworld.vn or call 0948 30 8888 the day before.
- Local spirits: táo mèo is wine made from the wild 'cat apple' of the Hoàng Liên range, served as shots or warm and spiced. Rượu ngô (corn wine) is a spirit, so sip it, ideally with thắng cố. Avoid unlabelled home-distilled rượu in plastic bottles, because methanol poisoning is a recurring problem; buy sealed, labelled bottles (about 150–300k VND).
- The valley cafés (Sailing Sapa, Lá Dao) are 8–12 km from town on steep roads, so treat them as stops on the Lao Chải–Tả Van trek. Carry cash for the area ticket (75k VND adult in 2025) and book a taxi (about 200–300k VND one way) or xe ôm back rather than walking uphill at dusk. Tả Van homestays host song-and-dance evenings mostly for groups, so ask when you book.
- Happy hours: the Bamboo Lounge at Bamboo Sapa Hotel (18 Mường Hoa) runs buy-one-get-one on any drink 16:00–20:00 daily (Jul 2026), bistro.sapa (7 Mường Hoa) has half-price Vietnamese craft beer 17:00–20:00, and Local Pub does 2-for-1 cocktails. Many small bars are cash-only. The DJ lounges on Cầu Mây lane run late (AMG until about 03:00) but draw overcharging complaints, so check every item on the bill.
- Many listicles, including Sun Group's own sunparadiseland.com, still recommend places that Google marks closed: The H'Mong Sisters, Wooden Pub, the Hill Station Deli, Cafe in the Clouds and The Coóng at Cát Cát. Check the Google Maps status on the day.
- Ask before photographing performers, villagers or rituals. The khèn troupes and children who pose for photos expect a small tip, so carry small notes. For background on the music, the free Sa Pa Museum (2 Fansipan St; closed 11:30–13:30) displays khèn, flutes and Hmong, Red Dao and Giáy costumes.

## Phú Quốc

*Phú Quốc · island in the Gulf of Thailand, far south*

Phú Quốc's west coast faces the sunset, so the island's day pivots on golden hour: salt coffee or cold brew on Sao Mai Hill, a bespoke cocktail or a 19th-floor Kraken-themed rooftop at dusk, then fire dancers on the sand of Long Beach. After dark the music splits between the Trần Hưng Đạo strip's cover bands and beach DJs and the resort-scale spectacles of Sunset Town and Grand World, where fireworks go off every night. It isn't an all-night clubbing island and specialty coffee is scarce, but sea-view cafés, a few genuinely inventive cocktail bars and the island's own sim-berry wine make up for it.

### Phú Quốc: local tips

- Sunset sets the schedule: the west coast (Ông Lang, Dương Đông, Long Beach, Sunset Town) faces it, golden hour runs roughly 4:30–6:30pm and the sun sets around 5:15–6:15pm, so arrive 30–45 minutes early for a front table. Skies are clearest November–April; in the May–October monsoon expect rain-outs and rough seas, and some places open only in the dry season (Mango Bay's Beach Bar runs November–April).
- Plan by hub. The Dương Đông–Long Beach strip on Trần Hưng Đạo (Chuồn Chuồn, Tarapaca, Origin 1982, Bittersweet, Cheeky Traveller, Blue Monkey, OCSEN) is the walkable late-night zone; the Bãi Trường resorts (INK 360, Sailing Club) are 15–20 minutes south by car; Sunset Town and Bãi Khem are 30–40 minutes from Dương Đông; and Grand World is in the far north, an hour or more from Sunset Town, so don't attempt both show hubs in one evening. Use Grab or a taxi rather than a motorbike after drinking: distances are long and the roads are dark.
- A live-music crawl on the strip: happy hour at The Rabbit Hole Irish Bar (Hẻm 118, 3–7pm, buy two get one free), Cheeky Traveller's house band from about 7:30pm in the same alley, OCSEN's beach fire show around 10pm, then Blue Monkey's Vietnamese singers until 2am. After that, a May 2026 guide names House No.1 (58 Bạch Đằng, by the night market) as the after-midnight bar. Sunset Town's Teatro Club (EDM, house and hip-hop until about 2am) is the only proper nightclub, but its reviews are polarised.
- Sunset Town fires fireworks twice a night, and you can watch them free from the waterfront, VUI-Fest Bazaar or the seafront bars; you only need a ticket for the full 9pm Kiss of the Sea (no full show on Tuesdays). Book at booking.sunworld.vn or through OTAs, re-check times on the day (Sun Group's own pages are marketing and schedules shift), and allow extra time: after 7pm private cars can't use Sunset Town's main streets and electric buggies do the last stretch.
- Budget for '++': resort and hotel menus add service charge and VAT on top, roughly 13–16% (Le Bar adds 5% service plus VAT, OCSEN 8% service), while Chuồn Chuồn's prices include both. Benchmarks: local cafés 20–85k VND a drink, craft beer 80–120k, beach-bar cocktails 200–270k, rooftop cocktails from about US$15 at INK 360.
- Rượu sim, made from the rose-myrtle (sim) berry, is the island's sweet-tart wine. Taste it at a producer such as Bảy Gáo in Suối Mây (free or about 30–50k VND entry with a tasting; bottles roughly 150–500k) rather than at the night market, or try it in Le Bar's Sim Berry Martini. Sim nectar and syrup are non-alcoholic, so check the label.
- Specialty coffee is thin on the island, and researchers found no well-known local roaster. For espresso go to Chuồn Chuồn (Arabica blend) or Eric Kayser; otherwise order Vietnamese styles (cà phê muối or salt coffee, coconut coffee, bạc xỉu, egg coffee), best at Tarapaca, which also has power outlets for laptop days.
- Since July 2025 Phú Quốc has been a special zone (đặc khu) of An Giang province, so listings mix 'Kiên Giang', 'Dương Đông' and new ward names, and many 2026 SEO and resort-blog listicles still send readers to closed or moved venues (Rory's Beach Bar is now on the east coast; Aroi Dessert Cafe, WoodHouse Coffee and Lá CafeDeli are marked permanently closed). Navigate by venue name plus the Trần Hưng Đạo number or alley (Hẻm 118, Hẻm 124), and check recent Google reviews before a long ride.

## Saigon

*Hồ Chí Minh City (Sài Gòn) · the south*

Saigon is bigger, louder and later than Hanoi. Sock-filter coffee stalls that date back to 1938 sit a few streets from fine-robusta roasters, and cocktail bars hide up the stairwells of 1960s apartment blocks. Nights run from plastic-stool nhậu and rooftop bands to phòng trà ballads, jazz and techno.

### Saigon: Cafés (Cà phê)

Saigon runs on robusta: brewed slowly through a phin or strained through cloth socks, poured over ice with condensed milk and drunk on plastic stools, park lawns and the balconies of 1960s apartment blocks. Sock-coffee stalls dating back to 1938 still work alongside a serious specialty scene that champions Vietnamese fine robusta and Đà Lạt arabica, while hidden cafés fill old apartment blocks, alleys and riverside villas.

  - Tip: Come in the morning, when neighbourhood regulars fill the low stools, and order a hot cà phê sữa; the house version is finished with condensed milk and a little butter. Enter via alley 109, because some sites print just '36 Nguyễn Thiện Thuật'.

### Saigon: Bars (Quán bar)

Saigon drinks at every altitude and price: plastic-stool nhậu strips toasting 'một, hai, ba, dzô!' over iced beer and grilled snails, brewers who put jasmine and passion fruit into Vietnamese craft beer, and a cocktail circuit hidden up the stairwells of old apartment blocks and colonial mansions, with two bars on the extended Asia's 50 Best Bars 2026 list. Add the hotel rooftops where war correspondents once drank above the Opera House, and one night can run from 15,000-VND draught to a 350,000-VND rotavap cocktail.

### Saigon: Music (Âm nhạc)

Saigon's music runs on two tracks. One is deeply local: phòng trà lounges where star vocalists sing bolero and Trịnh Công Sơn ballads to a hushed, seated room, and the South's own đờn ca tài tử and cải lương, which young musicians are now carrying into bars. The other is a scrappy DIY scene of jazz rooms, indie gig bars and underground techno crews spread across District 1, District 3, Bình Thạnh and Thảo Điền, where most nights are small, cheap and announced only days ahead.

### Saigon: local tips

- How to order: cà phê đen đá is iced black, cà phê sữa đá is iced with condensed milk and bạc xỉu is mostly milk with a little coffee. Add 'nóng' for hot and 'ít ngọt' for less sweet. At sock-coffee (vợt) stalls the coffee is brewed ahead and kept hot, so it arrives almost at once.
- Go early for heritage coffee: regulars fill the sock-coffee stalls from about 6:30am, and Cheo Leo closes around 6pm. After dark, the Phan Đình Phùng sock-coffee stall runs 24/7, and Tiệm Cà Phê Linh at 1 Trương Định, by Bến Thành metro station, is open 7am–2am.
- Specialty bars keep daytime hours: MANKI breaks 12:00–13:30 and shuts at 5pm, GRANDMUM shuts at 5pm and Lacàph at 7:30pm, while Every Half stays open until 9:30pm. Book MANKI's seven seats, GRANDMUM's coffee omakase and Lacàph's workshops ahead.
- Taste fine robusta black, as a pour-over or espresso, not just arabica: Every Half's Cư M'gar lot won Gold at the Global Coffee Awards 2025. Buy traceable beans from the roasters as souvenirs, and skip the 'weasel' (cà phê chồn) coffee pushed at souvenir shops, which is often fake or flavoured.
- Coffee crawls: GRANDMUM (alley 82), Hummingbird (103bis), Hoff (170A) and Every Half (232/23) are all on Võ Thị Sáu, and The Workshop (no. 27) shares Ngô Đức Kế with Every Half's Đồng Khởi store (8–24).
- Addresses: since mid-2025 districts have been abolished and wards merged, so listings may say 'Sài Gòn ward' or 'Bến Thành ward' instead of District 1. Search by street and house number. An address like 109/36 Nguyễn Thiện Thuật means house 36 in alley 109; have Grab drop you at the alley mouth and walk in.
- Old apartment blocks such as 42 Nguyễn Huệ and 145 Nguyễn Trãi are still homes: keep your voice down in stairwells and don't photograph residents' doors. At 42 Nguyễn Huệ the lift costs 5k VND per person, refunded when you order, and it's busiest around 8pm.
- Carry small notes: heritage stalls charge roughly 10–35k VND a cup and some take no cards or QR, while apartment and specialty cafés charge roughly 30–120k a drink.
- Prices: local draught costs 12–50k VND a glass, craft pints roughly 80–140k and signature cocktails at the top bars 260–400k. Hotel and rooftop menus marked '++' add service charge and VAT. Happy hour is the way to save: ~99k cocktails at Layla and Summer Experiment, half price from mid-afternoon to sunset at Social Club, and a free drink if you win BiaCraft's coin toss (5–6pm).
- Walkable bar crawls: on Tôn Thất Đạm, Snuffbox (14, a Gatsby-style speakeasy opened in 2015), Nhau Nhau (89) and BOO SG (119, inside the wet market) are minutes apart. On Lê Thánh Tôn, pair Stir (alley 136) with Nê Saigon (109), the Saigon outpost of Hanoi's phở-cocktail pioneer. On Lý Tự Trọng, Heart of Darkness (31D), Summer Experiment (77–79) and RAW + Atelier (97B), the new bar from Drinking & Healing founder Jimmy Nguyen, are a short walk apart.
- The best cocktail bars hide on the upper floors of scruffy old apartment blocks (Stir, Summer Experiment, Snuffbox): trust the map pin, look for the stairwell or lift, and don't be put off by the entrance. Book Friday and Saturday tables at The Enigma Mansion, Stir and Layla; most bars take reservations and announce guest-bartender nights on Instagram or Facebook.
- Rooftops: sunset falls between about 5:30 and 6:15pm year-round and rail-side tables go first, so book and arrive by 5. Hotel rooftops and the party-led Chill Skybar (26th floor, AB Tower) expect smart casual: no tank tops or flip-flops. In the rainy season (roughly May–November) storms can shut open terraces, so the enclosed EON Heli Bar (52nd floor, Bitexco Financial Tower) and Blank Lounge (floors 75–76, Landmark 81) are the weatherproof views; check Blank Lounge's hours on Google Maps, because its old website now redirects to a gambling site.
- Historic-hotel crawl, about 15 minutes on foot around Lam Sơn Square: start at the Rex Hotel's 5th-floor Garden Bar (20% off drinks 5–7pm; use the 'Rex Hotel Garden Bar' map pin, because an older duplicate shows as closed), catch the 5:45pm cocktail trolley at Saigon Saigon in the Caravelle, move on to M Bar at the Majestic for the river and the 8pm band, and finish with a nightcap at the Park Hyatt's 2 Lam Son.
- Bùi Viện Walking Street is pedestrian-only on weekend evenings and packed from about 8pm. Go once for the spectacle, drink at the plastic-stool beer stalls (draught 20–50k VND), and skip the bucket-and-hookah club bars, where inflated prices and tab disputes happen. Never buy laughing-gas balloons (bóng cười): they're illegal, and an August 2025 police crackdown seized 348 cylinders.
- Nhậu etiquette: beer comes with a bucket of ice, and the table toasts together ('Một, hai, ba, dzô!') and drinks on the toast. Bottles are counted by how many were opened at your table, so leave unopened ones alone and check the count on the bill; street joints are mostly cash-only. When the bars close, 5KU Station (17 Thái Văn Lung) grills and pours until 4am.
- Getting home: Vietnam has zero tolerance for drink-driving, so take Grab or Xanh SM rather than a rented motorbike or an unmetered taxi. Motorbike phone-snatching is the main risk around Bến Thành and Bùi Viện, so keep phones off roadside tables. Since the 2025 ward mergers, addresses may read 'Sài Gòn Ward' or 'Bến Thành Ward' instead of District 1, so search Grab by venue name.
- Find the week's gigs and parties on Saigoneer's events page (saigoneer.com/saigon-events) and RA's Ho Chi Minh City guide (ra.co/guide/vn/hochiminhcity). Most local gigs sell pre-sale tickets through a Google Form in the listing, usually 50–70k VND below the door price. Club nights start around 10pm, fill after midnight and run to 5–6am, and some promoters cut the door to about 100k after 3:30am.
- How you pay for the music: cover-band bars such as Acoustic Bar charge no cover, so you just buy drinks. Jazz rooms and phòng trà add a per-person surcharge (phụ thu) or sell tickets: a cover including one drink at Saxn'Art, 300k–800k VND on an ordinary phòng trà night and 1–2M VND when a star headlines. Local band showcases cost 150–350k VND, often with a drink. Carry cash, as smaller music bars don't always take cards.
- Phòng trà etiquette: book a day or more ahead by phone or Zalo and ask for a central table. Shows run about two hours in Vietnamese with plenty of chat between songs. Pre-1975 song titles in the billing mean bolero/nhạc vàng, and 'nhạc Trịnh' means Trịnh Công Sơn. Dress smart-casual and keep filming to short clips: in May 2026, executives of several live-music brands were prosecuted for posting show recordings on YouTube without permission.
- Opera House planning: Lune's AO Show (18:00, about an hour, mostly weeknights) and HBSO (weekends at 20:00) share the 468-seat house and rarely clash, so you can see AO on a weeknight and HBSO at the weekend. Ignore older guides: Lune's Teh Dar now plays only in Hội An, and Làng Tôi moves to Hanoi from 3 Nov 2026.
- Getting around: Thảo Điền (now An Khánh ward) is the second music hub, 20–30 minutes by Grab from District 1, with Saxn'Art and Blue Monkey on the same street (Nguyễn Văn Hưởng) and Kanpai nearby. Since the 2025 ward mergers, addresses show new ward names, so give drivers the street and number. Grab and Xanh SM run all night, so skip the unmetered taxis touting at closing time. Vietnam's drug laws are severe and police raid clubs, so carry nothing.
- Đờn ca tài tử has no fixed tourist stage in Saigon. Your best bets are Saturday nights at Hai Tô Bar, the Gia Định club's Wednesday-evening sessions at the Bình Thạnh ward traditional house (6B Phan Đăng Lưu), and the weekend riverside buffet at Bình Quới Tourist Village (439k VND including the show). The HCMC History Museum also stages free hát bội (classical Southern opera) on some Sunday mornings, announced a few days ahead on its website.
- More live music: the Caravelle's Saigon Saigon rooftop (reopened 28 Sep 2026; band Mon–Sat 8–10:30pm, Sun 8–10pm) and the Majestic's M Bar both have nightly hotel-rooftop bands. Carmen (8 Lý Tự Trọng; nightly flamenco and Latin house band) and Cội (77–79 Lý Tự Trọng; jazz Thu and Sun from 8:30pm) are long-running but unconfirmed for 2026, so check before going. The free 'No Cover' original-music showcase at Mooney's (Alley 40 Bùi Viện) runs roughly monthly on Saturdays at 7pm, and Le Café des Stagiaires' Thảo Điền rooftop hosts free vinyl-only HCMC Soul Club nights.
- Big dates, Oct 2026–Mar 2027: Sat 17 Oct is a concert pile-up (Anh Trai Vượt Ngàn Chông Gai at The Global City on 17–18 Oct, plus several stadium shows), so expect gridlock and ride surges. In December, watch for the free Saigon Record Fair (6 Dec in 2025), the Saigon Latin Festival (3–7 Dec 2026, in Vũng Tàu), HOZO's riverside festival (27–31 Dec in 2025), NTPMM's year-end show and the Nguyễn Huệ New Year countdown. Trịnh Công Sơn tributes cluster around 28 Feb and 1 Apr. Buy on Ticketbox on release day and avoid Facebook resellers.
